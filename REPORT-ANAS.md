## The Artefact

CarFun is a carpooling web application created by a five-person team of Bath Spa University RAK students. It was built to solve a real and daily problem for the student community at Bath Spa Academic Centre RAK in Ras Al Khaimah, UAE. Students from across the city's residential areas make the same journey to campus every day. Most of them do it alone. CarFun connects them so they can share rides, split costs, and make the commute cheaper and more sociable.

The application is a fully working platform where students can register, set up a profile with their area and schedule, find or offer rides, request and accept seats, message their co-passengers through an in-app chat, receive live notifications, and even drop a pin on a map to share their exact pickup location. It runs at http://localhost:3000 during development and was built and tested end to end by the team over the course of the module.

Our five-person crew each brought a different discipline to the project. Dhovie Bryan Costimiano and Fareedi Danish Baloch built the technical application from the ground up. Geof Raneses and Kisha Romeo led the marketing strategy and research. I, Anas Imtiaz, was responsible for the media side of the campaign: the visual identity, the social media design direction, and the video content concept and production.

The campaign materials produced for CarFun include a branded set of Instagram posts and stories, five TikTok video scripts, and a library of captions and hashtags. These assets are stored in the project's `marketing/` folder and are ready for use across social platforms.

CarFun connects to the incubator theme on two levels. It addresses the financial strain on a specific social segment, university students, by reducing the cost of a non-optional daily activity. And it carries a sustainability dimension: shared journeys mean fewer cars on the road, less fuel burned, and fewer emissions produced on the same commute route every weekday. The UN Sustainable Development Goal 11 frames sustainable urban transport as a priority, and a product that measurably increases vehicle occupancy on a regular community route contributes to that goal in a practical way (United Nations, 2015). CarFun also has a clear revenue model through service fees, which means the sustainability is financial as well as environmental.

The intended audience is Bath Spa RAK students who make a regular commute from residential areas across Ras Al Khaimah to campus.

## The Development

### Ideation and Planning

My role in the ideation phase was about finding the feeling of the product as much as the function. Once the crew had landed on the carpooling idea through our early group sessions, the question I was thinking about was: what kind of product does CarFun want to be? How should it look, sound, and feel to the students we were designing for?

That question mattered earlier than most people expect in a project like this. If the visual and media identity of a product gets defined late, after the technical build, it often ends up as an afterthought that does not quite match everything else. I pushed to have early conversations about the brand before the development team started working, so that the design direction of the application and the campaign materials could come from the same place.

Those early conversations led to a set of creative decisions that shaped everything that came after. The dark background. The lime green accent. The editorial-style typography. The tone of voice across all the marketing copy. These were not arbitrary aesthetic choices. They were decisions grounded in who the audience was and what kind of product we wanted CarFun to feel like for them.

While the development team built the application and Geof and Kisha worked on the research and strategy, I was working in parallel on the media direction. I developed the visual concept for the brand, briefed the design decisions that informed the Instagram and TikTok assets, and developed the video content concepts that Kisha scripted in detail.

### My Contribution

My contribution to CarFun covers the visual identity of the brand, the design direction of the campaign assets, and the video content concept and scripting framework.

**Brand Identity and Visual Direction**

The first thing I had to decide was what kind of visual world CarFun should live in. The audience is students aged 18 to 25. They use Instagram, TikTok, and Snapchat. They are used to products that look like products, not like university administrative tools. They judge credibility partly on aesthetics, whether or not they are conscious of doing it. A platform that looks dated or generic will not be trusted the same way a platform that looks intentional and contemporary will be.

I chose a dark theme deliberately. Dark interfaces communicate confidence and modernity, particularly to a younger audience. They also look distinctive compared to almost every other university-adjacent tool, which tends to default to white backgrounds and institutional blues. CarFun needed to look like a choice, not an assignment.

The lime green accent colour came out of thinking about what colour would carry both the energy of the product and a sense of sustainability. Green is associated with ecology and good choices, but most greens in design feel either clinical or corporate. The particular shade I settled on, a sharp electric lime, feels alive and contemporary without losing that underlying association. It also photographs well on screen, which matters for any content that will be screenshotted and shared on social platforms.

For typography I selected Syne for headings and DM Sans for body text. Syne at high weights has a strong, almost aggressive quality that communicates confidence without being cold. It is the kind of typeface you see on brands that want to be taken seriously without being formal. DM Sans balances it with legibility and warmth at smaller sizes. Together they produce a hierarchy that feels like a real product rather than a student project.

**Campaign Asset Design**

Working with the direction from Geof and Kisha's research and strategy, I developed the visual approach for the Instagram posts and stories. The five post formats each serve a different purpose in the content calendar and needed to communicate their messages clearly within the Instagram feed environment, where a user makes a decision about whether to stop scrolling in under two seconds.

Each post uses the CarFun brand consistently: the same colour palette, the same typefaces, the same dark background treatment. Consistency across a feed is what builds brand recognition. If every post looks slightly different, the viewer never accumulates a sense of who is posting. CarFun's feed should feel like a coherent brand identity, not a random collection of images.

The hero post was designed to work as a standalone brand statement. Someone seeing it without knowing anything about CarFun should understand immediately what it is, who it is for, and why it matters. The statistics, the clear headline, and the call to action all work together toward that single goal.

The story formats are designed to work in the vertical full-screen environment, which is a completely different visual context from the square feed post. Stories are scrolled through quickly and are often consumed with sound off. The text hierarchy in the story designs carries all the meaning without relying on any caption, and the bright lime elements create stopping points that interrupt the thumb.

**TikTok Video Concepts**

For TikTok, the medium is video and the context is fast. The typical user on TikTok decides within the first two or three seconds whether to keep watching or scroll past. This means the hook is the most important single moment in any TikTok piece.

I developed five video concepts with Kisha writing the detailed scripts. My input was on the visual and pacing approach for each. The first script uses a POV format, which is one of the most effective TikTok structures because it creates immediate identification with a specific experience the viewer is likely to have had. The second is a screen recording tutorial, which works because it shows the product in use without requiring the viewer to imagine what it looks like. The third uses a direct question addressed to the audience, which drives comment engagement. The fourth targets drivers specifically, which allows for more precise targeting and a more focused message. The fifth is a fast montage format using the list of areas served, which is both informative and visually satisfying in quick-cut editing.

Across all five, the visual approach should feel native to TikTok, meaning it should not look like an advertisement that has been repurposed for the platform. The most effective TikTok content from student-targeted brands looks like it was made by someone in the same age group using the same kind of phone. That authenticity is the hardest thing to teach and the easiest thing to lose when production values get too high.

**Photography and Footage Direction**

The current campaign assets are designed graphics rather than real photography or video, which is appropriate for a prototype phase. As the project moves toward a real public launch, the most important upgrade to the campaign is real footage. I have planned a short shot list for a launch video that covers: a student checking their phone and requesting a ride, a driver seeing the notification and accepting it, a brief exchange of messages in the app, a passenger setting their map pin, and a car pulling away from a residential street in one of the key RAK areas. This footage, cut to about 30 seconds, would serve as the hero asset for both a TikTok launch video and an Instagram reel.

## Routes to Market

### Target Audience

The primary audience is BathSpa RAK students commuting from Ras Al Khaimah's residential areas. From a media perspective, this is an audience that lives on Instagram and TikTok. They are visual consumers who make trust decisions quickly based on how something looks and feels before they read into the detail. The campaign materials I developed are built with this in mind.

The secondary audience worth reaching through the media campaign is prospective students who have not yet started at BathSpa RAK. Commuting cost is a real consideration for students choosing a university or deciding whether to live in RAK. Seeing a platform like CarFun in their discovery content before they even arrive could make it part of how they plan their student life from the start.

### Market Context

The global ride-sharing and carpooling market was valued at approximately USD 185 billion in 2023 and continues to grow, driven in part by younger consumers who are comfortable with app-based peer-to-peer services and increasingly aware of the environmental cost of solo car travel (Statista, 2024). The university segment of this market is genuinely underserved, and a product with a strong visual identity and active social media presence has a real advantage in capturing it.

Student financial wellbeing research consistently identifies transport as a significant burden. Over 60% of UK university students ranked it among their top financial concerns in a 2022 NUS study (NUS, 2022). The financial pain point is real, and the media campaign is designed to speak directly to it.

### Monetisation

CarFun is free at launch, which is the right call. A service fee on ride contributions is the natural first revenue model once the community is established. From a media perspective, a subscription-free product is significantly easier to market to a student audience than one with upfront costs. The free positioning should be a prominent part of the campaign messaging at every stage.

Longer term, a branded content partnership with a local business that serves the student market could integrate naturally into the social media content without feeling intrusive. Advertising through the app's interface is also viable once the user base is large enough to make the placement meaningful.

## Evaluation

### Project and Digital Campaign

The overall output of this project is strong. The application works, the campaign materials are polished, and the brand has a clear and consistent identity. The thing I am most proud of is that the app and the campaign look like they belong together. That does not happen by accident. It happened because we talked about visual direction early and the development team took the design decisions seriously when building the interface.

What the campaign needs next is video. Everything I have described in the TikTok section exists as scripts and concepts. The production has not happened yet. Getting those five videos filmed and edited would transform the campaign from a well-designed set of static assets into an active, growing presence on the platforms where our audience spends their time.

The recurring content challenge after launch is staying authentic. The easiest mistake a student-targeted brand can make is producing content that looks like it was made by a marketing department rather than by people in the same community. Keeping the voice genuine, using real student stories, and giving the audience reasons to engage rather than just consume are the things that determine whether a social media presence grows or stagnates.

### Team Collaboration

Working with Geof and Kisha on the campaign was the most productive creative collaboration I have had on a university project. They brought the research and the strategy, I brought the visual direction, and the combination produced something better than any of us would have made separately. The briefing process was mostly informal, which worked because we were in close enough contact for misunderstandings to surface quickly. A slightly more structured creative briefing document would have made the workflow cleaner, but the output was strong regardless.

Working with the development team from the other side of the table was a different experience. Dhovie and Fareedi were building something I was designing around, without access to the codebase. That meant I had to be clear about what I needed the product to look like when translating design direction into assets, and I had to be flexible when the product's final form was slightly different from what I had anticipated. Both of those are useful disciplines for working in a multi-role creative team in any industry context.

### Individual Development

This project pushed me to think more systematically about brand identity than I had before. I am comfortable with visual design at the execution level: layout, typography, colour, hierarchy. What I developed during this project was a stronger sense of how those visual decisions connect to strategic goals. Choosing the dark theme was not an aesthetic preference. It was a decision about how CarFun would be perceived by the audience, what trust signals it would send, and how it would stand out in a feed full of student-facing content. Making that argument clearly in team conversations helped me understand my own decisions better.

On the media production side, I learned the difference between having a strong concept and having a concept that is actually producible with the resources you have. Some of my early video ideas were visually ambitious in ways that would have required equipment and time we did not have. The process of scaling back to what could be done well with what we had improved both the practicality and the honesty of the final concepts.

On collaboration, I want to work on being more vocal earlier. I tend to develop ideas internally before bringing them to the group, which means I sometimes present things at a stage where they are harder to change. Sharing rougher, earlier thinking invites better feedback and produces stronger outcomes. That is something I want to apply differently in the next project.

CarFun as a product and a brand is something I am genuinely glad I was part of. It has a clear identity, a real audience, and a strong foundation. What it needs now is to be seen.
